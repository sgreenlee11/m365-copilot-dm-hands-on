From store signal to district action

Welcome and objectives

This is a practical lab for District Managers and multi-store field leaders. You will copy, adapt, and use Microsoft 365 Copilot prompts built around the district operating rhythm: read what is happening across your stores, walk into store visits prepared, find where your time matters most, and turn priorities into owned store actions. Use your own work context when possible; fallback demo data is fictional.

Catch up

Read the district across email, Teams chats, channels, and meetings—grouped by store.

Prepare

Walk into a store visit or store manager check-in with commitments, metrics, and coaching questions ready.

Analyze

Use the scorecard and customer feedback to find the stores, and the risks, that need you this week.

Cascade and follow through

Turn district priorities into a store action plan, then track owners and dates from the district call.

Training data note: All fallback demo data describes District 07 of Northwind Home Improvement Co., a fictitious 10-store district used for training purposes only. Store names, managers, and results are invented.
Activity 2

The prompting pattern

Strong prompts give Copilot a clear goal, relevant context, the sources it should use, and clear expectations for the response.

GoalContextSourceExpectations

Worked example

Identify the store in my district that most needs my attention this week and prepare me for a check-in with its store manager. I lead a multi-store district. The check-in is 20 minutes, and the objective is to agree on no more than two priorities and how I can support the store. Review email and Teams chats from the past two weeks where I am a sender or recipient that mention a store, store manager, customer escalation, staffing, safety, or operational issue. Cite the messages supporting each finding. Begin with a brief explanation of why you selected this store. Then provide three things I should know, one question to ask the store manager, one barrier I could remove for the team, and anything I should verify before the conversation.
  • State the goal clearly: what do you want Copilot to accomplish?
  • Add context about your district, the store, the audience, or the time period.
  • Identify the email, chat, meeting, file, or report Copilot should use.
  • Set expectations for format, length, and what to prioritize—short enough to read between stores.
  • Verify facts, numbers, and names against the system of record before acting or sharing.
Activity 3 · 7 minutes

Catch up: read the district.

Use Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat to scan what came in from your stores, partner teams, and leadership, then sort it into what needs you and what your store managers should own.

Start a new chat before running this exercise so context from the worked example does not influence the response.
  1. Set your scope. Replace the bracketed area with your district name, store numbers, or the partner teams you work with.
  2. Run the prompt. Review the sources Copilot provides and open at least one supporting item.
  3. Challenge the result. Ask what is uncertain, contradictory, or missing before you act on it.

District pulse

Review my email, Teams chats, and meetings from the past five business days related to my district and stores [district name or store numbers]. Group what you find by store. For each store with activity, tell me what changed, whether it needs my action, the store manager's action, or is FYI only, and cite the source. Then list the three items across the district most likely to affect customers, associates, or results this week, and flag anything that has been waiting on me for more than two days. Separate confirmed facts from possible implications.

Pressure-test follow-up

Which of these items has the weakest evidence or conflicting information? Tell me what I should confirm with the store manager before acting.

Optional · Catch up on a district Teams channelOpen your district or store managers' channel in Teams, open Copilot, and paste the prompt below.

Channel catch-up

Summarize the past week in this channel by store. List requests waiting on me, open questions nobody has answered, and every deadline mentioned with the person responsible.

Reflection: Did Copilot show you where to spend your time, or only what arrived?

Activity 4 · 8 minutes

Prepare: walk in ready.

Before a store visit or store manager check-in, bring together what has changed, what was committed last time, and what to look for on the walk—so the visit is spent coaching, not catching up.

Start a new chat before this exercise so the preparation is grounded only in the store and the content you add.
Point Copilot to the right context: In Copilot Chat, make sure Work IQ is on. Select Add work content in the prompt box, or type /, then add the store manager by name. Optionally add the store's latest scorecard or visit notes as a file. Each item appears as a reference in the prompt.
  1. Choose a real store you will visit or a store manager you will meet soon, and add them to the prompt using the steps above.
  2. Run the visit brief prompt with the references attached.
  3. Verify commitments and metrics against your visit notes and reporting before treating them as fact.

Store visit brief

I am visiting [store name or number] on [day], and the store manager is [add the store manager with /]. Using my email, Teams chats, meetings, and any file I added, prepare a one-page visit brief. Include what has changed at this store in the past 30 days; commitments made at my last visit or since, with owner, due date, and status; the three metrics or conditions I should check on the walk and where in the store to look; two coaching questions for the store manager; and one thing the team has done well that I should recognize. Do not infer anyone's performance or intent unless the content supports it, and mark anything uncertain as Verify on the walk.

Activity 5 · 12 minutes

Analyze: find the store that needs you.

Move from messages to evidence. Choose one route; each uses a different District 07 dataset and a prompt built for the question a District Manager asks every week: where will my time make the biggest difference?

Copilot in ExcelUse the district workbook directly in Excel to rank stores on results and operating health.

  • Download and open the District 07 workbook in Excel.
  • Select the Copilot button near the upper-right corner to open the Copilot pane.
  • Start a new conversation in the pane, then paste the prompt below.

Copilot in Excel prompt

Analyze the Store Scorecard sheet to show where district leadership attention would have the greatest impact this week. Rank the ten stores on the most recent week and on their six-week trend across sales vs plan, on-shelf availability, pickup on-time, customer satisfaction, staffing coverage, shrink, and safety incidents. Identify any store that looks healthy on sales but carries an operational risk, and any data-quality issues I should not trust. Show the evidence behind each finding, create one chart that makes the most important comparison clear, and recommend the three stores to prioritize with the first action for each.

AnalystUse the Analyst agent in Microsoft 365 Copilot to find where store operating decisions are showing up in customer feedback.

  • Open the Microsoft 365 Copilot app and select Analyst under Agents or Tools.
  • Select the + attachment control and add the District 07 customer feedback. For a richer analysis, also add the district workbook.
  • Start a new Analyst conversation, then paste the prompt below.

Analyst prompt

Analyze this customer feedback for the ten stores in District 07. Group comments into recurring themes, quantify each theme by store and department, and show where feedback worsened during the period. Identify where a store's operating decisions may be creating a customer problem, and if the store workbook is attached, show where the comments confirm or contradict the scorecard. Include representative comments as evidence, recommend the three store-level priorities with the expected customer benefit of each, and clearly identify the limitations of survey and review data.

Optional · Bring your own data

Analyze the attached [store scorecard, labor report, or survey export] to help me decide [where to focus my store visits, labor, or coaching this week]. The data covers [time period and stores]. First describe the fields and any data-quality limitations you observe. Then identify the most important trends, exceptions, risks, and opportunities by store; support each finding with evidence; and recommend three actions. Clearly distinguish what the data proves from what I would need to confirm in the store.

Pressure-test either route

Argue against your top-priority store. What alternative explanation, missing data, or store context could change where I should spend my time?
Required final step: After reviewing and correcting the analysis, run the handoff prompt below. This creates the common input used in Activity 6, regardless of which route or dataset you chose.

Create the Activity 6 handoff

Create a District Priority Handoff from this analysis. Include: the priority question; the three stores to prioritize and why; the evidence for each; the first action and suggested owner for each store; one best practice from a strong store that others could adopt; two risks or counterarguments; and unresolved questions. Keep it under 350 words. Mark any unsupported or uncertain item as Needs validation.

Keep the tone direct, supportive, and specific, with no corporate jargon. Preserve important numbers and retain every Needs validation label.

Activity 7 · 5 minutes

Follow through: hold the line.

Point Copilot to your last district call or store manager meeting, turn the discussion into store-by-store commitments, and open a ready-to-review follow-up in Outlook.

Start a new chat before adding the meeting so Copilot does not carry forward context from the analysis and writing activities.
Add the specific meeting: In Copilot Chat, make sure Work IQ is on. Select Add work content, or type /. Choose Meetings, search by meeting title or organizer, and select the meeting before pasting the prompt. No transcribed district call? Download the District 07 call transcript and attach it with + instead.

Store commitment tracker and Outlook follow-up

Using the meeting or transcript I added to this prompt, create a store-by-store commitment tracker from the district call. For each store, list decisions made, action items with the named owner and stated due date, and any support requested from me or a partner team. Then list district-wide decisions, unresolved questions, and items discussed but not decided. Do not invent an owner or deadline; mark it as Unassigned or Not specified. Then draft an Outlook email to the attendees with district decisions first, the store tracker second, and open questions last. End by asking recipients to correct anything that does not match their understanding.

Make it field-ready

Make the email concise and field-ready: a clear subject line, under 250 words, easy to read on a phone, and a reminder of when we will review progress.
  1. Review the generated email card in Copilot Chat and confirm the recipients, subject, decisions, owners, and dates.
  2. Select Open to continue editing the generated draft in Outlook.
  3. Edit and send only after confirming the draft against the meeting record.
Availability note: Use a meeting that was transcribed or recorded so Copilot can ground the tracker and follow-up in the meeting content. The demo transcript works for practice when you do not have one.
Activity 8 · 5 minutes

Lead responsibly.

Copilot accelerates preparation and follow-through. Accountability for what happens in your stores remains with the leader who acts on the output.

VerifyOpen sources and confirm names, dates, and numbers against the system of record.
SeparateDistinguish facts, interpretations, assumptions, and recommendations.
ProtectKeep associate and customer information in approved tools and within its intended permissions.
ChallengeAsk for counterarguments, missing evidence, and the store context the data cannot show.
CoachUse Copilot to prepare conversations, not to judge people. People decisions stay with you and your HR partner.
OwnThe plan, the visit, and the follow-through remain yours—not Copilot's.
